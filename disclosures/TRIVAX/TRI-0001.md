## 1. Technical problem

Many inference, classification, anomaly-detection, and signal-processing tasks use learned high-dimensional parameter spaces. For edge systems this can imply memory, compute, calibration, and energy costs that are disproportionate to the dimensionality of the control/decision problem.

TRIVAX explores a lower-dimensional resolutive representation in which observations are projected into a compact structured state and decisions are obtained from deterministic geometric/state relations instead of requiring a large neural network at inference time.

This disclosure records the architectural principle and current implementation lineage. It does not claim universal replacement of neural networks or superiority across arbitrary datasets.

## 2. Definitions

- **Resolutive state:** compact machine state used for deterministic decision mechanics.
- **Axis/coordinate:** one component of the compact state representation.
- **Projection:** deterministic mapping from observation/features to a resolutive state.
- **Decision region:** subset of the resolutive space associated with a class/action/regime.
- **Interference/coherence relation:** deterministic relation among state components used to distinguish or score candidate outcomes.
- **Calibration:** process that fixes parameters/thresholds before deployment.

## 3. Technical solution

TRIVAX separates feature acquisition from a compact resolutive decision core:

```text
observation
    |
feature extraction / normalization
    |
compact resolutive projection
    |
TRIVAX state
    |
geometry / interference / thresholds
    |
decision or score
```

The decision core is intended to remain small enough for CPU/edge execution and to expose its decision mechanics explicitly rather than through a large opaque parameter matrix.

The implementation may evolve in dimensionality and exact equations; what is disclosed here is the architectural use of a compact structured resolutive state as the primary inference space.

## 4. Disclosed technical features

1. Compact resolutive inference state separated from raw input dimensionality.
2. Deterministic decision mechanics over the compact state.
3. Explicit calibration parameters and thresholds rather than mandatory end-to-end deep-network inference.
4. Suitability for low-power edge deployment and online adaptation experiments.
5. Reproducible benchmark orientation with comparisons against conventional statistical or learned baselines.
6. Separation between the TRIVAX decision core and any upstream feature extractor.

## 5. Minimal algorithmic model

```text
TRIVAX_INFER(x):
    f := normalize_or_extract_features(x)
    s := project_to_resolutive_state(f)
    scores := evaluate_state_relations(s)
    y := deterministic_select(scores, calibrated_thresholds)
    return y, scores
```

A conforming disclosure must specify the concrete projection and relation functions before claiming a frozen implementation.

## 6. Worked example

For a low-dimensional sensor-control problem, a raw sensor vector is normalized and mapped into a compact state. The engine evaluates its relation to calibrated decision regions and returns the corresponding action. No LLM is required and a large neural network is not intrinsic to the decision stage.

## 9. Failure modes and limitations

- A compact representation may discard information needed by a task.
- Calibration can overfit a benchmark.
- Performance may degrade under domain shift.
- Upstream feature extraction can dominate total compute even when the TRIVAX core is small.
- Low parameter count does not by itself prove better generalization, energy efficiency, or accuracy.
- Claims comparing TRIVAX with neural networks require matched datasets, latency targets, hardware, and quality metrics.

## 10. Related work and prior art

Relevant classes include linear/nonlinear classifiers, finite-state systems, fuzzy systems, reservoir/low-dimensional dynamical systems, kernel methods, probabilistic classifiers, and compact neural models. The intended distinction is the resolutive-state formulation and deterministic relation-based decision core used across the Resolutive Technology family.

A formal literature and patent prior-art survey remains required.
