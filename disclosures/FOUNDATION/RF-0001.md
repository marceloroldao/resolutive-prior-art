## 1. Purpose

This disclosure establishes a common architectural foundation for the Resolutive Technology family. It records the shared technical concepts used across Resolutive Memory / Memoria.ia, MA2A, Resolutive Computing, Resolutive Inference, TRIVAX, trajectory systems, and hierarchical bit-relation analysis.

This document is a defensive technical disclosure. It defines mechanisms and interfaces at the family level without asserting that every experimental claim has been validated or that every described component has reached production maturity.

## 2. Technical problem

Conventional intelligent and autonomous systems frequently depend on repeated high-dimensional inference, verbose message exchange, centralized processing, and reconstruction of context from large payloads. These characteristics can increase latency, bandwidth, memory movement, computational cost, and energy consumption, particularly in battery-constrained robots, drones, embedded systems, IoT devices, and distributed autonomous agents.

The Resolutive architecture investigates an alternative organization in which persistent state, deterministic state relations, trajectories, compact addresses, and local resolution are first-class computational objects. Neural or language models may remain optional components rather than mandatory transport or memory mechanisms.

## 3. Foundational definitions

### 3.1 State

A state `S_t` is a finite machine-readable representation of a system at logical step `t`.

### 3.2 Transition

A transition is a deterministic or explicitly parameterized mapping

`T(S_t, u_t) -> S_(t+1)`

where `u_t` is an input, event, symbol, observation, control value, or relation used by the transition.

### 3.3 Trajectory

A trajectory is the ordered relation

`Tau = (S_0, S_1, ..., S_n)`

or a compact representation sufficient to identify or reconstruct relevant relations among these states. Information may therefore be associated not only with isolated stored values but with transitions and relations between states.

### 3.4 Resolutive address

A resolutive address is a stable key or derived identifier that maps a recognized state/relation/trajectory class to a resolution route. The implementation may use hash tables, indexed structures, compact state signatures, trajectory identifiers, or equivalent deterministic structures.

### 3.5 Resolution route

A resolution route is a previously established path from an observed or addressed condition to an action, memory entry, next state, candidate trajectory, or delegated resolver.

## 4. Core architecture

The family architecture separates four logical operations:

1. **Observation/encoding:** transform an input into a finite state or relation representation.
2. **Addressing:** derive or locate a resolutive address associated with that representation.
3. **Resolution:** retrieve or execute the corresponding route without requiring a new general-purpose inference over the entire historical state whenever a known route exists.
4. **Evolution:** if no adequate route exists, invoke an external learner, search process, human, peer, neural model, or other resolver; the resulting route may subsequently be recorded.

Conceptual pseudocode:

```text
function resolve(observation):
    state = encode(observation)
    address = address_of(state)

    if route_table.contains(address):
        return execute(route_table[address])

    result = external_resolution(state)
    if validation_policy(result):
        route_table[address] = compile_route(result)
    return result
```

The architecture does not require `external_resolution` to be neural. It is an extension point.

## 5. Memory as executable routing structure

Resolutive Memory treats memory retrieval as route resolution rather than solely semantic reconstruction. A known address can reference a stored state, transition, trajectory, action, or another address.

For an indexed implementation with bounded-key lookup, expected lookup can be O(1) under the assumptions of the selected data structure. This complexity statement applies to address lookup, not automatically to encoding, collision handling, validation, network transport, arbitrary semantic search, or execution of the retrieved route.

This distinction is essential: the architecture does not claim that arbitrary intelligence or arbitrary semantic reasoning is O(1).

## 6. Hierarchical relation processing

Raw data may be transformed into relations at successive granularities. A lower layer identifies local patterns or relations; an upper layer operates on the outputs of the previous layer.

Example abstraction:

```text
raw symbols
   -> pair/local relations
   -> relation blocks
   -> higher-order relation classes
   -> trajectory/state address
   -> resolution route
```

The block sizes and relation operators are implementation choices. The family includes experiments over bit/byte sequences and hierarchical relation layers.

## 7. Reversible and verifiable trajectories

A trajectory mechanism may maintain boundary states and verification material, for example:

`Record = {initial_state, final_state, step_count, verifier}`

where `verifier` may be a cryptographic hash or another integrity check. A reversible transition family can permit backward traversal when the transition parameters are known. The verifier confirms reconstruction but does not itself contain the reconstructed information.

No claim is made here that arbitrary data can be losslessly compressed below information-theoretic limits by storing only a fixed-size hash and a step count. Any compression claim requires separate empirical and mathematical evidence.

## 8. Local-first distributed architecture

A distributed Resolutive system can be organized into three logical layers:

- **L1 — Edge:** local device executes sensing, state encoding, resolutive memory, control, rendering, and optional local AI inference.
- **L2 — Peer/mesh:** devices exchange permitted state or trajectory information directly when useful.
- **L3 — Coordination:** a lightweight service routes permitted trajectory/state deltas, validates synchronization policy, coordinates shared state, and persists authorized shared information.

A central L3 service is not required to execute an LLM. High-cost inference may remain at L1 or be delegated explicitly.

## 9. State/trajectory synchronization between agents

Agents that share a compatible representation can synchronize compact state identifiers, trajectory identifiers, deltas, versions, and validation material rather than repeatedly exchanging a natural-language reconstruction of their entire context.

Conceptual message:

```text
SYNC {
    agent_id,
    namespace,
    trajectory_id,
    base_version,
    delta,
    verifier,
    policy_scope
}
```

Natural language may still be used at human-facing boundaries. The architectural objective is to avoid requiring natural language as the internal transport representation between compatible agents.

The detailed negotiation, identity, privacy, conflict resolution, and synchronization semantics belong to the MA2A specification.

## 10. Privacy boundary

Every state or trajectory entry intended for distributed synchronization must have an explicit scope. A minimal model is:

`scope ∈ {private, local, shared, global}`

A synchronization layer MUST NOT infer permission merely from technical reachability. Entries marked `private` or `local` remain outside shared synchronization unless an explicit policy changes their scope.

## 11. Online accumulation

A device may accumulate validated routes during operation:

`unknown condition -> resolver -> validated result -> route registration -> subsequent direct resolution`

This permits a hybrid architecture in which expensive inference is concentrated on unresolved conditions while previously validated conditions can use deterministic routes.

## 12. Example

Consider a battery-powered autonomous device observing a sensor pattern `P`.

At first encounter:

```text
P -> encode -> A7F2 -> no known route
                |
                -> external resolver -> action R
                -> validation
                -> store A7F2 -> R
```

At a later compatible encounter:

```text
P -> encode -> A7F2 -> R
```

The second path does not require the same external resolver if the stored route remains valid under current policy and context.

## 13. Family mapping

This foundation is specialized by separate disclosures:

- **RM:** deterministic/resolutive memory and routing.
- **MA2A:** synchronization and communication between compatible agents.
- **RI:** compact inference and decision mechanisms.
- **RC:** computational search/resolution mechanisms.
- **TRI:** TRIVAX state/relation mechanisms.
- **RT:** reversible and trajectory-oriented information mechanisms.
- **BA:** hierarchical bit/byte relation analysis.
- **RP:** separate physics hypotheses; these are not required for the computing architecture to operate.

The separation of RP is deliberate: experimental computing mechanisms must remain independently testable without requiring acceptance of a physical ontology.

## 14. Novel combination being disclosed

The disclosed architectural combination is the use of persistent deterministic state/trajectory addressing as a common substrate for memory, route reuse, local-first autonomous operation, and compact agent synchronization, with expensive or neural inference treated as an optional resolver for unresolved states rather than a mandatory mechanism for every retrieval or inter-agent exchange.

Individual ingredients such as finite-state machines, hash indexing, caching, distributed synchronization, cryptographic verification, and neural fallback are known techniques. This disclosure concerns their specific organization within the Resolutive family and does not assert exclusive invention of those pre-existing primitives.

## 15. Limitations and unresolved questions

- Semantic encoding may dominate computational cost even when route lookup is constant-time on average.
- Address collisions and distribution shift require explicit handling.
- Previously valid routes can become stale or contextually unsafe.
- Distributed conflict resolution requires version and authority semantics.
- Privacy and identity require cryptographic and policy enforcement.
- Claims of energy reduction require hardware benchmarks.
- Claims of compression require comparison against established compressors and information-theoretic analysis.
- Claims of improved inference require controlled external baselines.
- The architecture does not establish AGI, universal reasoning, or universal O(1) computation.
