## Scope

This document records a foundational mathematical/experimental research line associated with Resolutive Physics. It is **not** a statement that Resolutive Physics is an established physical theory, nor that all broader ontological interpretations are experimentally confirmed.

## Core disclosed model class

A related public implementation studies coupled fields including an Abelian-Higgs sector and a neutral scalar field, with vortex/interface solutions and a shifted critical boundary. The broader Resolutive research vocabulary also investigates phase, layered organization, trajectory/state relations, and multi-scale coupling as hypotheses to be mapped to testable models.

A generic coupled-field representation may be written as a Lagrangian density containing field-gradient terms and an interaction potential,

`L = kinetic(fields) - V(fields, couplings)`,

with model-specific equations and parameters defined by the referenced implementation rather than inferred from philosophical terminology.

## Disclosed features

1. Use of explicit coupled-field mathematical models as testable realizations of broader Resolutive hypotheses.
2. Separation between ontology, mathematical model, numerical experiment, and empirical claim.
3. Vortex/interface solutions and critical-coupling investigations as a reproducible research line.
4. Requirement that each physical claim identify its observable, dataset, baseline, free parameters, and falsification criterion.

## Reference evidence

The public `coupled-field-vortex-model` repository includes a frozen V0.1 scientific validation campaign and an archived V0.1.0 release. Its citation metadata records DOI `10.5281/zenodo.21936796` and describes a coupled Abelian-Higgs/neutral-scalar vortex model with perturbative critical-boundary displacement, interface tests, and vortex validation.

## Limitations

- Numerical agreement in a selected model does not validate a universal physical ontology.
- Cosmological, gravitational, quantum, atomic, or time interpretations require separate disclosures and independent tests.
- Parameters fitted to data must not be described as derived constants unless independently derived.
- Claims from simulations must be distinguished from laboratory/observational measurements.
- This registry records disclosure chronology, not scientific acceptance.
