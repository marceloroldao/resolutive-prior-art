## 1. Technical problem

An open specification and open/reference implementation do not by themselves distinguish the official commercial network from unauthorized or independently operated networks. MA2A-0003 discloses a hierarchical public-key infrastructure in which an organization receives an official network identity and may issue subordinate credentials to its own devices/agents, while official L3 services accept only identities chaining to the configured MA2A trust root and satisfying current license/network policy.

The purpose is network admission, identity, revocation, and trust. It is not a claim that cryptography can prevent independent forks from creating their own roots and networks.

## 2. Roles

- **MA2A Root Authority:** top-level trust anchor for the official network.
- **Organization Authority:** licensed company/organization identity certified by the MA2A Root.
- **Organization Memoria.ia:** central organizational service holding or controlling the organization authority key/material and policy.
- **Device/Agent Identity:** subordinate Ed25519 key pair and certificate/credential issued or authorized by the organization.
- **Official L3 Verifier:** service that validates certificate chain, signature, status, protocol capabilities, and policy before admitting a session.
- **Admission/status service:** authoritative source for organization and device/agent admission state such as ACTIVE, SUSPENDED, EXPIRED, or REVOKED.

## 3. Hierarchy

```text
MA2A OFFICIAL ROOT
        |
        +-- Organization A certificate
        |       |
        |       +-- central Memoria.ia
        |       +-- device/agent 1
        |       +-- device/agent 2
        |       `-- device/agent N
        |
        +-- Organization B certificate
        `-- Organization C certificate
```

The commercial relationship is primarily organization-level. The root does not need to directly provision every device.

## 4. Cryptographic primitive

Ed25519 is the initial signature primitive because it provides compact public keys/signatures and widely available implementations. Protocol negotiation must include an algorithm identifier so future versions can migrate or add approved algorithms without silently changing semantics.

Each private key must remain under the control of the corresponding authority/device. The protocol never transports private keys.

## 5. Organization certificate

A logical organization credential contains at least:

```text
OrganizationCertificate {
    certificate_version
    organization_id
    organization_public_key
    issuer_id
    valid_from
    valid_until
    network_id
    allowed_capabilities
    policy_version
    serial_number
    issuer_signature
}
```

The exact production encoding is not frozen in v0.1. The public reference implementation supplies a deterministic logical certificate representation and signed canonical payload sufficient to reproduce the disclosed trust flow.

## 6. Device credential

A subordinate device/agent credential may contain:

```text
DeviceCertificate {
    device_id
    organization_id
    device_public_key
    issuer = organization_id
    valid_from
    valid_until
    capabilities
    serial_number
    organization_signature
}
```

Organization policy may choose short-lived credentials, hardware-backed keys, or delegated sub-authorities. The archived v0.1 release candidate demonstrates one Root -> Organization -> Device/Agent delegation level and does not claim arbitrary-depth PKI support.

## 7. Handshake

The minimum challenge-response flow is:

```text
Client -> L3: HELLO
    protocol_version
    organization_id
    device_id
    certificate_chain
    client_nonce
    capabilities

L3 -> Client: AUTH_CHALLENGE
    server_nonce
    challenge_id
    issued_at
    expires_at
    selected_protocol_version
    selected_capabilities

Client -> L3: AUTH_RESPONSE
    signature = Ed25519.Sign(device_private_key,
        canonical(protocol_version,
                  organization_id,
                  device_id,
                  client_nonce,
                  server_nonce,
                  challenge_id,
                  selected_capabilities))

L3:
    verify root -> organization -> device chain
    verify certificate validity periods
    check organization status
    check device/agent status
    verify delegated capability
    verify signature and challenge freshness

L3 -> Client: AUTH_OK or AUTH_DENIED
```

Binding both nonces, challenge identity and negotiated capabilities to the signed transcript reduces replay and cross-session substitution risk.

## 8. Admission predicate

Conceptually:

`ADMIT = chain_valid AND signature_valid AND fresh_single_use_challenge AND org_status_ACTIVE AND device_status_ACTIVE AND capability_allowed AND policy_valid`

A cryptographically valid certificate is therefore necessary but not always sufficient for official network admission.

## 9. License/network-status integration

Organization and subordinate device/agent state are modeled independently from certificate cryptographic validity:

```text
ACTIVE
SUSPENDED
EXPIRED
REVOKED
```

This permits policy to disable a compromised device without revoking the whole organization and to suspend an organization without pretending its historical signatures cease to be mathematically valid.

The reference verifier receives current statuses from the admission layer; distributed persistence and propagation of revocation state remain infrastructure responsibilities.

## 10. Public executable evidence

The MA2A `v0.1.0-rc1` release is archived at DOI `10.5281/zenodo.22048589`. Its executable Ed25519 reference implementation covers:

- Root-signed organization certificate issuance and verification;
- organization-signed device/agent certificate issuance and verification;
- certificate validity intervals and certificate-version/algorithm checks;
- organization/device identity binding to the challenge;
- single-use challenge replay detection;
- separate organization and device/agent ACTIVE-status enforcement;
- delegated capability enforcement;
- canonical transcript signing and verification;
- wrong-root and wrong-delegation rejection;
- altered nonce/capability transcript rejection;
- expired certificate/challenge rejection;
- malformed Base64/public-key/signature inputs failing closed;
- seeded mutation/fuzz-style tests over certificate signatures and challenge transcript fields;
- green GitHub Actions test execution on Python 3.11 and 3.12 before merge of the baseline.

The DOI identifies the archived release snapshot and does not include later `main` commits. This is reproducible experimental evidence, not an independent cryptographic audit or production-security certification.

## 11. Revocation

Revocation can occur at multiple levels:

- root rotation/emergency distrust;
- organization certificate revocation;
- device certificate/status revocation;
- capability revocation;
- commercial/network admission suspension.

A compromised device should be removable without revoking the entire organization. A compromised organization authority may require replacement of its subordinate trust tree.

The public security review explicitly records that the current reference verifier's replay cache is process-memory-only and that persistent/distributed revocation storage is not yet implemented by the reference package.

## 12. Official versus independent networks

An independent implementation can create:

```text
ThirdParty Root -> ThirdParty Organization -> devices
```

but that chain does not validate against `MA2A Official Root` unless explicitly cross-authorized.

Thus:

`protocol compatibility != official network membership`

and:

`source availability != possession of official trust credentials`.

This distinction is a central commercial and security property of the architecture.

## 13. Privacy and minimization

The handshake should expose only identity metadata necessary for authentication, authorization, routing, auditing, and policy. It must not require private memory payloads or LLM prompts. Device identifiers may be pseudonymous/rotatable where business and audit requirements permit.

The security review also records that metadata itself can reveal organizational/device relationships and timing patterns, so production deployments require data-minimization and retention policies.

## 14. Failure modes and attacks

Threats include:

- stolen private keys;
- replayed challenge/response;
- forged or substituted certificate chains;
- stale revocation data;
- organization authority compromise;
- root compromise;
- downgrade to weaker protocol/algorithm versions;
- confused-deputy authorization between organizations;
- cloning a legitimate device credential to multiple devices;
- denial of service against status/validation infrastructure.

Mitigations include nonce freshness, signed capability negotiation, short-lived credentials, hardware-backed keys where appropriate, audit logs, rate limits, key rotation, and multi-party/root operational controls.

## 15. Commercial boundary

The PKI is not defined as a mechanism to calculate royalties or inspect customer financial data. Commercial royalty/accounting obligations are contractual. PKI answers a narrower question: whether an identity is authorized to participate in the official trust/network domain and with which capabilities.

The public MA2A license and commercial-licensing note further separate copyrighted official materials from abstract protocol ideas and separate protocol availability from MA2A Official Network admission, certification and trademark rights.

## 16. Worked example

A robotics manufacturer signs an MA2A commercial agreement for a product line. The MA2A Root signs an organization certificate for the manufacturer's central Memoria.ia. The manufacturer provisions subordinate device certificates to robots in that licensed ecosystem.

When a robot reaches an official L3 relay, it presents its chain and proves possession of its private key with the challenge-response handshake. L3 validates the organization and robot as ACTIVE and admits the session. If a single robot is compromised, that device credential/status can be revoked without disabling the manufacturer's entire fleet.

## 17. Limitations and unresolved questions

The archived public baseline does not yet provide production implementations for:

- Root key custody ceremony/HSM/KMS integration;
- persistent distributed challenge/replay storage;
- revocation/status propagation SLA and cache semantics;
- certificate-transparency mechanism;
- key rotation/cross-signing/federation profiles;
- transport confidentiality/channel binding (e.g. TLS profile);
- production clock-skew policy;
- final compact certificate/wire encoding;
- independent security audit.

These do not invalidate the disclosed mechanism; they limit the level of production-security assurance that may be claimed.

## 18. Related work

Relevant comparison classes include X.509 PKI, WebPKI, SSH certificate authorities, SPIFFE/SPIRE identities, mTLS service identity, device-fleet PKI, certificate transparency, OAuth-style authorization layers, and signed software/network entitlements.

MA2A-0003's disclosed combination is organization-level official-network certification, delegated device identity, cryptographic challenge-response, separate organization/device admission policy, and integration with the resolutive-agent architecture.
